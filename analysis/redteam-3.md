# Red-team review 3 — the practitioner's objection

**Reviewer persona:** senior environmental lawyer, twenty-five years split between a state agency and a national environmental organisation. NSR, Title V, NPDES, ESA. Hostile by assignment.

**Target:** `src/content/essays/the-latency-bet.md` (draft, 2026-08-03).

---

This is a much better piece than the last one you sent me. The proxy-stack framing is now respectful of the drafting instead of contemptuous of it, and the Part 75 paragraph is the correct read. So I am going to spend my time on the load-bearing claim, because if it fails the essay is an observation rather than an argument.

## 1. "The gaps are irreducible" — FATAL as stated

Your central move is this: *"no amount of redrafting eliminates that set. You can move it. You can shrink one and widen another. You cannot close it, because closing it would mean writing a rule with no proxy in it, and a rule with no proxy in it is a sentiment."*

That sentence is false, and the counterexamples are inside the corpus you practise in.

40 CFR 63.4(b) — the circumvention provision of the Part 63 general provisions — prohibits building, installing or using any article, machine, equipment or process *the use of which conceals an emission that would otherwise constitute noncompliance*. There is a parallel provision in the NSPS general provisions at 40 CFR 60.12. Neither one specifies a measurable quantity. Both are enforceable. Both are aimed at exactly the conduct you say the system structurally cannot reach: satisfying the number while defeating the point. CWA §404(f)(2) does the same thing in the wetlands programme — the recapture provision strips an otherwise-available exemption when the activity is part of a scheme to convert the area to a new use. That is a rule that turns on purpose, not on tonnage. NPDES has 40 CFR 122.45(f) and (h), which require mass-based limits and forbid meeting a limit by dilution, closing an entire class of substitution exploits by prohibiting the *method* rather than by tightening the *number*. And EPA's anti-sham project-aggregation position in NSR exists precisely because someone once fragmented a project to stay under a threshold.

Then step outside. Tax faced your problem earlier, harder, and with better-funded adversarial search, and it did not respond by moving to physical grading. It responded with a purposive backstop: the sham-transaction and economic-substance doctrines out of *Gregory v. Helvering*, codified at 26 U.S.C. §7701(o) with a strict-liability penalty at §6662(b)(6), plus general anti-abuse rules in the UK, Canada, Australia and ATAD Article 6. You may think codified economic substance is unadministrable. Say so and show your work. What you cannot do is write *"That leaves exactly one move"* while a live, deployed, cross-jurisdictional fifth option sits unmentioned. Exhaustion claims are falsified by a single survivor.

The honest reformulation is available to you and it is weaker: a purposive backstop is enforced by a human institution at human speed, so what asymmetric search actually threatens is not the *existence* of anti-circumvention machinery but its *throughput*. That is a real and interesting claim. It is also a different claim, and it admits a remedy you have ruled out — accelerate the enforcer rather than replace the grading substrate. Your product interest sits uncomfortably close to that omission.

Related, and nearly as bad: *"the reported number is the only layer that anything automated ever sees."* The credible evidence rule (62 Fed. Reg. 8314, Feb. 1997, amending 40 CFR 51.212, 52.12, 60.11(g), 61.12(e)) exists to sever that identity. Any credible evidence may establish a violation; the reference method is not the exclusive proof. Add CAA §113(c)(2) for false statements and monitor tampering, and the Part 70 responsible-official certification of truth, accuracy and completeness at 40 CFR 70.5(b), and the permit shield's limits at §504(f) — which shields nothing you failed to identify and nothing you misrepresented to get. The reported number is not the terminus you describe.

## 2. The opening NSR scenario — SERIOUS, and one leg is simply wrong

I expected to catch you here and mostly could not. The free choice of any consecutive 24-month period in the preceding ten years is real for non-EGU units under 40 CFR 52.21(b)(48)(ii), a different period may be selected for each unit, and nothing forbids reusing a period picked in 2019 for another purpose. The "representative of normal source operation" test you might have been caught by belongs to the pre-2002 regime and to the EGU alternative-period provision. So: lawful, as you say.

But the heater. You describe *"the projected actual emissions from the new equipment."* A new emissions unit has baseline actual emissions of zero and its post-change figure is potential to emit; the actual-to-projected-actual test applies to existing units, with the hybrid test allocating each accordingly. Whatever the 24-month baseline is doing in your story, it is not being applied to the new heater. Either the project is a modification of existing units — in which case say so — or the paragraph is wrong on the face of the rule, and every NSR practitioner who reads it will stop there.

Two omissions make the scenario too neat. First, baseline actual emissions must be adjusted downward to exclude emissions that would have exceeded a limit the source must currently comply with — the favourable high period is not free. Second, you cite 40 CFR 52.21(r)(6) nowhere. The reasonable-possibility recordkeeping rule requires pre-project documentation of the projected actuals and the excluded demand growth, and five to ten years of post-project tracking and reporting. Your four days do not vanish into a memo. They generate a discoverable record that an agency can read back against actual post-project emissions. That is the system doing to you exactly what you say it structurally cannot.

## 3. Candour or self-exemption — SERIOUS

*"Nothing about that is improper."* That sentence is legally correct and rhetorically load-bearing, and it will not land the way you want. You describe four days of work that kept a refinery project out of BACT review, bill it approvingly (*"It was worth what they paid"*), and then reclassify the professional capacity that produced it as *"not legal reasoning. That is search."* Practitioners will notice the sequence: the author performs the conduct, declares it proper, devalues the skill that made it possible, and arrives at a remedy in a market he discloses selling into. The disclosure is genuinely good and unusually placed. It does not cover this.

The fix is cheap and you should take it. Say what the heater emitted. Say what BACT would have required. You are asking a general audience to accept that the gap is structural rather than moral; you make that case by showing the cost you imposed, not by asserting propriety and moving on.

## 4. The deregulatory guard — SERIOUS, incomplete perimeter

The guard is better than anything else in this genre and I want that on the record. It also protects the wrong perimeter. It defends permit conditions and fixed limits. It says nothing about the thing your essay actually arms: *"Better rules, faster rulemaking, more enforcement staff, sharper penalties—each of them operates on the first term of the product, and the first term is not what changed."*

That is a budget argument. Handed to an appropriations examiner, it says enforcement headcount is structurally futile. It will be cited in NSR-reform comment letters, in AI-preemption briefs arguing states cannot out-draft the technology either, and in vendor decks. Your guard does not reach any of them, because it only forbids loosening limits. Extend it: continuous physical grading is funded *in addition to* inspectors and rulemaking capacity, never in substitution, and a claim that enforcement is futile is a misreading of a claim that enforcement is rate-limited. Also note the defensive point you left on the table — dense continuous data strengthens citizen suits under CAA §304 and CWA §505 rather than displacing them.

## 5. What it gets right — grudgingly

Part 75 substitute data as *pricing* rather than permission is the correct and rarely-stated read. The averaging period as the health endpoint — ozone at eight hours, SO₂ at one — is right and almost nobody outside the programme offices says it. "Federally enforceable as a practical matter" is the distinction that separates people who have written synthetic minors from people who have read about them. The netting paragraph correctly identifies the creditability step as the thing that does the work. The refusal to let ecology supply values is the best paragraph in the piece and inoculates you against the failure mode that ruins this entire genre. The frontier-lab values-document audit is novel, checkable and damning. The three dated falsification conditions are real ones, and the fact that you specify what would prove you wrong is worth more than the argument it defends.

## OBJECTIONS THE AUTHOR CANNOT ANSWER

The claim that proxy gaps are irreducible is contradicted by anti-circumvention rules already in force. 40 CFR 63.4(b) and 60.12 prohibit concealment without specifying a measurable quantity; CWA §404(f)(2) recaptures an exemption on the basis of purpose; 40 CFR 122.45(h) forbids compliance by dilution. These are enforceable rules that turn on intent rather than on a number, which is the category the essay says cannot exist.

The assertion that only one move remains is falsified by the tax system's response to the same problem. Codified economic substance under 26 U.S.C. §7701(o), and general anti-abuse rules in four other jurisdictions, are purposive backstops operating inside a proxy stack. Their existence means the essay must argue they fail rather than proceed as though they were never tried.

What asymmetric search actually threatens is the throughput of anti-circumvention enforcement, not its existence. That is a claim about institutional capacity, and it admits the remedy of accelerating the enforcer — a remedy the essay rules out without addressing.

The credible evidence rule already severs the identity between the reported number and the enforceable fact, so the reported number is not the sole layer visible to an automated system.

The recordkeeping obligations at 40 CFR 52.21(r)(6) convert the baseline choice into a documented, trackable, reportable position rather than an invisible one, which undercuts the claim that the search product leaves no enforceable trace.

The essay's boundary section protects permit limits but not enforcement appropriations, and the sentence declaring more enforcement staff structurally ineffective is the most citable line in the piece for a hostile reader.

The opening scenario declares the conduct proper and never states the emissions consequence, which reads as exemption rather than confession.

## FACTUAL CORRECTIONS REQUIRED

1. **New heater / baseline mismatch.** A new emissions unit has baseline actual emissions of zero; its post-change value is potential to emit. The 24-month baseline selection applies to existing units under the actual-to-projected-actual test, with the hybrid test governing mixed projects. Recast the scenario as a modification affecting existing units, or the paragraph is wrong on the rule.
2. **Ten-year lookback is unit-class specific.** 40 CFR 52.21(b)(48)(ii) gives the ten-year window to non-EGU units; existing electric utility steam generating units get five years, with an alternative period only on a reviewing-authority determination. Add the qualifier.
3. **Mandatory downward adjustment.** Baseline actual emissions must exclude emissions that would have exceeded a currently applicable limit. The favourable high period is not free-standing.
4. **Cite 40 CFR 52.21(r)(6).** The essay's own argument requires acknowledging the reasonable-possibility documentation and post-project tracking regime.
5. **Part 75 reporting cadence.** Part 75 requires hourly *recording* and *quarterly* electronic reporting, publicly available through EPA's Clean Air Markets data. "Required hourly reporting, publicly posted" overstates the cadence of public availability.
6. **The ~96% coverage figure** needs a source in the ledger and a stated denominator (fossil generating capacity, generation, or affected units — they differ).
7. **"The gaming stopped being worth it"** is too strong. Part 75 has a QA/QC enforcement history including falsified relative accuracy testing. The correct claim is that the payoff moved from estimation judgment to instrument fraud, which is a felony and much rarer.
