The Dawes Act disbanded the reservations (assimilation was the policy objective at the time). In 1934, FDR signed the Indian Reorganization Act--which reestablished the tribal boundaries. During the interim, land was sold to non-tribal people. The result . . . checker-boarded reservations (sometimes referred to as "fee-land").  How do I know this? Why do I know this? How did this become relevant?  Steve Jobs took calligraphy in college for some strange reason  . . . and that's why computers have such beautiful font. I for some strange reason found myself taking a course in Native American History. I've got no idea why.  After college, and a brief stint on a firefighting crew for the National Park Service in the Black Hills, I went to work for the state environmental department in Pierre. My job had nothing to do with the tribes. But we were encountering a problem. When a state environmental inspector showed up, the land owner would say "you're on tribal land . . . get off". Then when a tribal inspector showed up, the same landowner would say "you're on fee-land (state land), get off".
